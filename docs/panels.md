# Panels

The dashboard **Taut: Celestia validator** has up to 13 panels: a strip of four at the top that answers "is everything fine right now", then the groups Validator and signer, Fibre and Hosts. The playbook builds it from your inventory: the signer panels appear only with `taut_signer: remote` (the group is then called Validator and signer, otherwise Validator), and the Fibre group and the Fibre load tile only with `taut_fibre_enabled: true`. With a local signer and Fibre off, the dashboard has 7 panels. Dashed red lines sit at the alert thresholds in [alerts.md](alerts.md). With more than one host, each host keeps one colour across the panels.

Every graph carries a marker each time systemd starts celestia-appd, Fibre or tmkms: "<unit> started on <host>". A stop adds no marker. Markers can be missed on ranges longer than about a week, where Grafana takes coarser steps.

## Now

- **Node at chain head**: Is the node following new blocks, or stuck, syncing, or watching a halted chain, and is it peered? Three tiles: new blocks in the last 3 minutes (red at 0, which is what NodeNotAtHead alerts on), block sync (orange "yes" while the node catches up) and peers (red at 0).
- **Services per host**: Are celestia-appd, Fibre and tmkms running, and how often did systemd restart them? One tile per unit: green "active" or red "not active", and restarts in the last 24 hours (orange from 1). Only automatic restarts under `Restart=` count; a manual stop and start does not.
- **Link load (busiest minute, last hour)**: Is any host's link close to full, in either direction? One bar per host and direction: the busiest 1-minute average in the last hour, against `taut_link_mbps` or, when that is not set, the NIC port speed. Orange from 70%, because 1-minute averages hide sub-second bursts, and red from 90%. Every physical NIC counts; VPN, container, bridge, bond master and VLAN interfaces are left out. "speed unknown: set taut_link_mbps" means the NIC reports no speed (common on VMs): set `taut_link_mbps` for that host. Port speed can be above what your provider lets through, so set it too when your plan is below the port speed. A full link shows up as upload timeouts and late votes; the fix is a bigger plan or port, or moving Fibre to its own host.
- **Fibre load vs limit**: How close is this Fibre server to its storage limit, and so how busy is the Fibre network? The shard bytes the server holds over its stake-based budget, with a sparkline over the dashboard range; orange from 90%, and the storage limiter rejects uploads (`budget_exceeded`) at 100%. Shards are kept for the shard-retention window (4 h by default). The budget is the full-stake limit (2 TiB per window by default, about 146 MiB/s) times this validator's share of rows, and every blob gives the validator that same share, so the reading follows the network's Fibre data in the window: rejections near 100% come from network load, not a local fault. Each blob also adds proofs and a 64 KiB RLC, about 2.2 MB at full stake and 3.9 MB at the 148-row minimum, so small blobs read high (a 1 MiB blob counts as 3-5 MB) and low-stake validators reach the limit first. "No limit" means the budget is unlimited, or the validator was not in the active set when Fibre started. The reading falls to 0 while the validator is out of the active set.

## Validator and signer

- **Blocks since my last signature**: Is my validator signing right now? Near 0 while it signs; a line that climbs means blocks pass without its signature, and the dashed line is where ValidatorNotSigning fires. It works after restarts and jails, when missed-block counters freeze. The line stops at 10 times the ValidatorNotSigning threshold (500 blocks by default): right after a node start the value is the full block height until the first own signature, and one restart would otherwise flatten the chart. The alert uses the uncapped value.
- **Signer latency vs 10 ms**: Am I within Celestia's 10 ms median requirement, and is consensus or Fibre (`raw_bytes`) signing getting slower? The median of the last 50 signatures must stay under the dashed 10 ms line; the p99 lines per message type show which kind of signature slows down. The median appears after 50 signatures since the node started and stays frozen while none succeed.
- **Signing failures by reason**: Which signing path fails? Empty on a healthy setup. `no_connection` or `timeout`: the KMS host or the link; proposal only: the KMS cannot propose; `raw_bytes` only: tmkms too old for Fibre. Every retry counts as a failure, so a short outage shows as a burst.

## Fibre

Panel links open the official [Fibre](https://github.com/celestiaorg/celestia-app/blob/v10.4.0/observability/docker/grafana/dashboards/fibre.json) and [Fibre runtime](https://github.com/celestiaorg/celestia-app/blob/v10.4.0/observability/docker/grafana/dashboards/fibre-runtime.json) dashboards for per-RPC and Go runtime detail, and [Tensile](https://tensile.huginn.tech) and [Valopers](https://testnet.celestia.valopers.com/fibre/providers) for the view from outside.

- **Time since last successful upload**: Is Fibre serving, not just running? A sawtooth that drops back with each upload is healthy; a line that climbs past the dashed threshold (ten uploads at this server's 7-day average rate, never under 1 hour) is FibreNotServing. It counts from Fibre's start until the first upload, and has no threshold line when the server had no upload in 7 days.
- **Shard requests**: Are uploads and downloads arriving and succeeding, is the storage limiter refusing uploads, and are uploads slow? Requests per second by `success`; rejected uploads by reason (the storage limiter refusing uploads); upload p95 latency on the right axis.
- **Fibre disk**: Will the Fibre disk hold the stake-based storage budget? Occupancy near the budget is normal at full load; worry when the free space is smaller than the budget minus occupancy (DiskLow). A budget of 0 means no limit: Fibre found no stake for the validator.

## Hosts

Panel links open [Node Exporter Full](https://grafana.com/grafana/dashboards/1860-node-exporter-full/) for everything else about a host.

- **Network traffic**: What is using each host's link over time, and how much of it is Fibre? Traffic of the whole machine, not just Celestia: receive above zero, transmit below, as 5-minute averages (the Link load tile has the 1-minute peaks). With Fibre on, dashed lines show the Fibre shards accepted and served: shard row data that was signed, including re-uploads of a shard already held, without proofs, RLC or rejected uploads. They are drawn over the NIC line, not subtracted from it; the gap covers consensus P2P, RPC, row proofs and RLC, TLS, and rejected uploads. If Fibre fills the link, size the link up or move Fibre to its own host; if something else does, look for snapshot or state-sync serving, or RPC load.
- **Memory per host**: Is Fibre or the node running out of RAM? Available memory per host; OOM kills in the last hour on the right axis. Fibre can use up to about 27 GiB at its default connection limits ([connection caps and memory](https://github.com/celestiaorg/celestia-app/blob/v10.4.0/fibre/cmd/README.md#connection-caps-and-memory)); any OOM kill means the kernel killed a process, and `journalctl -k` names it.
- **Free space per data disk**: Which data disk is close to full or read-only? One bar per configured mount: red below 3% free, orange below 10%, green above (the DiskLow thresholds); the read-only tile turns red on "yes".
